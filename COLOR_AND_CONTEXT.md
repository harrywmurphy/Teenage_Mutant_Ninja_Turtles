## The naming (origin material)

- The names came from **Laird's copy of Janson's *History of Art*** — the standard college art-history textbook supplied the names that later rewrote the canon's public face (The Week, retrospective feature) [v]
- Japanese names were tried first: "we couldn't think of authentic-sounding Japanese names" (Laird); the Renaissance names "felt just quirky enough to fit the concept" (Laird, via Mental Floss oral history) [v]
- **Donatello was almost Bernini.** Eastman wanted Bernini, his favorite sculptor; Laird argued Donatello "rolled off the tongue better" (NBC News, 2020) [v] → analyzed as the almost-treated counterfactual (see below)
- In-canon, Splinter names the turtles from **a ruined book of Renaissance art found in a storm drain** — an art history book in a sewer
- The red bandanas (pre-1987, all four) were a homage to *Skateman*, a comic the creators considered one of the worst of its era (Laird) [v]
- Scale of origin: Mirage Studios was "no actual studio, only kitchen tables and couches with lap boards" (Eastman); first print run 3,000 copies funded by a $500 tax return, $200 in savings, and $1,300 from Eastman's uncle — the niche dose our break test correctly declines to find in any corpus

## Creator sourcing round 2 (added Sep 17)

- **Exogeneity testimony:** creators consistently describe the naming as casual — Japanese names failed, Janson's was on the shelf, euphony decided Donatello over Bernini. Treatment assignment was near-arbitrary → strengthens the natural-experiment design; cite in methods
- **Spelling correction dated:** Eastman & Laird corrected "Michaelangelo" -> "Michelangelo" at the start of Mirage Volume 4 (2001), after the misspelling appeared in ALL prior TMNT media (Turtlepedia) [v] — gives the fossil's decay a documented cause
- **Laird based Donatello's personality on himself** (Donatello was his favorite; en-Wikipedia, cited) — the trait now dominating the sculptor's name-space is the co-creator's self-portrait
- Eastman explains the naming on camera (YouTube short, Kevin Eastman) [v — locate original interview]
- Print-run discrepancy: 3,275 copies / $1,000 uncle loan (Wikipedia, cited) vs 3,000 / $1,300 total (Mental Floss) — prefer former, footnote the conflict

## The Bernini counterfactual (analysis, done)

- Books: Donatello/Bernini frequency ratio **0.73 pre-treatment → 0.78 today** — no divergence; Bernini out-books Donatello now as he did in 1980
- Web: the order flips — Donatello leads in pageviews (26.8k vs 22.9k/mo) and language editions (103 vs 77) despite being the less-written-about artist
- One sentence: *Bernini is what Donatello would look like if Laird had lost the argument.* (Caveat: Baroque vs quattrocento; a narrative counterfactual, not a formal control)

## The art world leans in

- **The Met, Jan 2018:** invited costumed turtle-Michelangelo to "Michelangelo: Divine Draftsman and Designer" as a photo op; tweeted that its "celebrity guest came to see the work of an artist who happens to share his name" (Artnet; Time Out) [v]. Our pageview check: no visible bump on the artist's article (Jan 2018: 175k vs 171k-184k surrounding months) — even a Met blockbuster + turtle stunt is noise next to the franchise's own doses
- **NBC News, 2020:** asked the National Gallery's senior curator of prints and drawings, Jonathan Bober, which four artists *he* would have picked for the turtles — a senior curator adjudicating the invented quartet; the piece frames a season of exhibitions (Getty's Leonardo "Mind of the Master," the NG's Verrocchio, the Frick's Bertoldo) through the TMNT lens [v]
- **Pedagogy inverted:** Teachers Pay Teachers sells lesson plans introducing the Renaissance *via* the turtles ("We Aren't Just Ninja Turtles — Renaissance Painters Jigsaw"); the NJ Italian Heritage Commission publishes a four-lesson unit that "starts with the cartoon characters and then introduces the Italian masters." The turtle is now the on-ramp to the canon
- **Museum institutionalization of the franchise itself:** Springfield Museums' "TMNT and Samurai Heroes" (2016–17); the Woodman Museum's permanent "Birthplace of the Ninja Turtles Gallery" (Dover NH, 2024, with Kevin Eastman Studios); the Smithsonian holds TMNT material in its collections [v]
